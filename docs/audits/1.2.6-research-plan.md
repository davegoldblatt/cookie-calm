## Scope of this audit

I read only the five files you pasted. `annoyance-rules.js` (`CATEGORIES`, `CANDIDATES`, `RULES`), `registration-prompts.js`, `survey-prompts.js`, the guarded runner, and whatever calls `Interactions.noteChange` were not available, so anything below that depends on them is marked conditional.

Your root-cause diagnosis checks out against the code. `structuralContainers` filters on `CLOSE|DECLINE` before it will even emit a container, so discovery dies first; and for a structural container `classify` sets `evidence = promptText(container)`, whose default exclusion list contains `a,button,[role="button"]`, so both the opener span and the close button are stripped and `CATEGORIES.find` sees an empty string. Adding the label alone genuinely does not fix it.

## Concrete risks

**1. Putting `close teaser` in the shared `CLOSE` is the widening, not the predicate.** `CLOSE` is consumed by `structuralContainers`, by `actionKind` for *every* category and every reviewed rule, and by `adblockChoice`. Your stated mitigation — "any container with `Close teaser` that fails the contract stops classification" — only holds if that veto is a top-level early return in `classify()`, ahead of the rule branches (`reviewedNotice`, `reviewedDismissal`, `rule.context`) and ahead of the `CANDIDATES` path where `evidence = text` (button labels included). If it lives inside the new predicate, a container with a `Close teaser` control plus non-button prose matching the offer pattern is classified and actioned by the ordinary generic path without the predicate ever running. "Teaser" is also standard vocabulary for article/video previews, so this is not a hypothetical label collision.

Simpler and strictly safer: **do not touch `CLOSE`.** Add a separate `TEASER_CLOSE`, union it into `structuralContainers`' discovery filter, and honor it in `actionKind` *only* when the category is the new teaser category. Then no rule path, no `adblockChoice`, and no generic-offer path can ever act on that label, and the stopping veto becomes unnecessary rather than load-bearing. This is fewer moving parts than "widen vocabulary + add a veto that must outrank every other branch."

**2. Registration/survey vetoes sit ahead of where your predicate lands, and they silently eat the common variant.** `classify` does `if (REGISTRATION_INTENT.test(text) && !registration && !optionalAdblock) return '';` where `text` includes button labels but `evidence` (passed to `isRegistrationPrompt`) is the button-stripped `promptText` — empty for this container. So a teaser reading "Sign up & save 20%" or "Get 25% off — join" almost certainly returns `''` before your predicate is reached. Your fix covers "25% OFF" and not the signup-worded teaser, which is at least as common in Klaviyo. Don't reorder to fix this; that is unsafe widening. Accept the gap, and add the signup-worded teaser as an explicit *negative* fixture so the boundary is deliberate.

**3. `permits()` protection is permanent, not 10 seconds.** Adding `offer` to `['registration','adblock','privacy-notice','survey']` means any qualifying container hits `this.protect(container, key)`, which adds to `this.protected` and `this.keys` with no expiry. Worse, `fingerprint` for this container yields `generic:offer:<aria-label|id|category>` — no heading, likely no `aria-label`, and if `id` is empty the key degrades to `generic:offer:offer`, shared by every generic offer on the page. One unrelated gesture within the 10 s window permanently poisons that key for the whole page lifetime, not just this teaser. Separately, `record()` already adds a category to `this.categories` when any clicked button's text matches a `CATEGORIES` pattern — on a commerce page, "Save 20%" or "Get $10 off" upsell buttons plausibly match the offer pattern and permanently block all offer dismissals. Widening the persistent guard here is not the cheap conservatism the plan implies; the plan's phrase "10 sec persistent interaction guard" understates it.

**4. `openedByUser` never expires either.** `noteChange` gates recording on a 10 s window, but `intentNodes` is a `WeakSet` with no time check and `openedByUser` walks the whole ancestor/host chain. Your "full popup → teaser" lifecycle fixture must distinguish node **reuse** from node **replacement**: if Klaviyo re-shows the same teaser node that was marked during the popup close, it is suppressed forever, and "replace beyond 10 sec" only passes because a fresh node was allocated. Assert both outcomes explicitly. (Conditional — I could not see the caller of `noteChange`, so which nodes get marked is unverified.)

**5. The rotated container interacts badly with completion verification.** `PromptCompletion.bounds` uses `getBoundingClientRect`, which returns the inflated axis-aligned box for a rotated element. `settled()` then treats any mutated, visible `fixed`/`sticky` node overlapping that box as `remaining` and withholds completion indefinitely. On a Shopify storefront a sticky header or sticky add-to-cart bar along the same viewport edge that mutates during the action window (scroll class toggle, cart count) is enough to make a successful dismissal report as failure, burn both `available()` attempts, and look like the fix doesn't work. Test this before the live baseline, not after.

Relatedly: use `offsetWidth`/`offsetHeight` (untransformed border box) for the predicate's compactness ceiling, not `getBoundingClientRect` — otherwise the rotation inflates the measurement and rejects the exact target you're building for. Your observed 50×184 is presumably the untransformed size.

**6. Apply the structural vetoes to hidden nodes too.** Klaviyo pre-mounts the full signup form in the same subtree in some configurations. If "no form/fields/frames/editables" is scoped to *visible* nodes, you admit a container that is one state flip away from being a form. Keep the control *count* visible-scoped (that's your two-control contract) but make the form/field/iframe/media/shadow vetoes presence-based via `querySelector`, matching how `protectedForm` is deliberately stricter than it looks.

**7. "No other instructions" must not reuse `promptText`'s default exclusions.** Those drop `header,footer,aside,nav` as well as buttons, so instruction text inside a `<header>` within the teaser is invisible to the veto. Use a dedicated walker in the style of `adblockAuthRequest`, which exempts only one narrow control shape and keeps everything else.

**8. The 160-control slice happens before the `CLOSE` filter.** `structuralContainers` truncates `querySelectorAll(PROMOTION_CONTROLS)` to 160 in DOM order and only then tests labels. Index 81 of 89 is fine on the page you measured, but a Soylent product page with the cart drawer open, or any heavier template, pushes the teaser past the cap and the fix silently stops working. You flagged this as "not a blocker"; I'd call it an acceptance risk for this specific change. The cap exists because `label()` calls `innerText` and forces layout, so don't just raise it — add a cheap pre-filter on `aria-label`/`textContent` before the expensive `label()`/`visible()` calls.

**9. Opener shape brittleness.** `PROMOTION_CONTROLS` is `button, [role="button"], a:not([href])`. A second-host variant whose opener is `<a href="#">` is not a control at all, so "exactly two controls" becomes one and the contract fails closed. That's safe but means the second-host fixture may be testing a shape that doesn't generalize. Your "no links" veto already covers the widening direction; just document that href openers are out of scope rather than discovering it in the field.

**10. Verify the runner's hit-testing tolerates transformed ancestors.** This is the first target under a CSS rotation. If the guarded runner does an `elementFromPoint` sanity check at the element center, confirm it survives the rotation and any `overflow` clipping on the parent. Conditional — I don't have the runner.

**11. Practical: Klaviyo frequency suppression is per-profile.** After the first successful native close, Klaviyo's own suppression (the behavior in the help-center article you cite) prevents the teaser from reappearing. Each live trial needs a fresh disposable profile or a targeted clear of Klaviyo's `localStorage` keys, or you'll read "no teaser" as "dismissed."

## Is the contract general enough without unsafe widening?

The predicate itself is narrow and I don't see it admitting anything dangerous. It is *under*-general in three documented places (signup-worded teasers per #2, href openers per #9, and vendors that mount the form in-subtree per #6), and those are acceptable. The unsafe widening in this plan is not in the predicate at all — it is in the two shared mechanisms: `CLOSE` (#1) and `permits()` (#3). Fix those two and the risk profile is fine.

## Simpler safe alternatives

- **Preferred: `TEASER_CLOSE` instead of widening `CLOSE`** (#1). Same coverage, materially smaller blast radius, and it removes the need for the "stops classification" veto to outrank every branch in `classify`.
- **Reviewed rule instead of a new predicate.** The codebase already has exactly this machinery: `RULES` entries with `container`/`control`/`controlLabel`/`action`, plus single-attempt semantics via `(rule?.reviewedNotice || rule?.reviewedDismissal) && this.attempts.has(key)`. A vendor-class rule (`[class*="kl-teaser" i]`) is hostname-agnostic in the sense your plan cares about, requires no change to `CLOSE`, no change to `classify`, and no new predicate to maintain. Cost, stated plainly: it's vendor-coupled and breaks on a class rename, and it doesn't generalize to other teaser vendors. If the honest goal is "unblock Soylent now, generalize later," this is the smaller change and I'd weigh it seriously against the structural predicate rather than dismissing it on the no-hostname-selectors principle — a vendor class is not a hostname.
- **Not viable, and you were right to reject them:** CSS hiding (`appHideEligible` is deliberately app-store-banner-only) and clicking the opener.
